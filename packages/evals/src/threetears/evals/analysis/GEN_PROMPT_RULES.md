# Writer-prompt rule ledger

**For:** maintainers editing `gen_prompt.py` (`EVAL_ANALYSIS_GEN_DEFAULT`, the analysis writer's seed prompt).
**Answers:** what each numbered rule is there to prevent, so a rewrite does not drop a rule without knowing what
it cost to learn.

**How to use it.** The prompt is budgeted at `PROMPT_CHAR_BUDGET` (24,500 characters) and `PROMPT_RULE_BUDGET`
(13 rules), and both ceilings only fall; `tests/test_gen_prompt_budget.py` fails past either. A rule is a numbered
entry under the `RULES` heading; the paragraphs above it are not counted. A change that adds a rule names the rule
it replaces or absorbs, and edits this table in the same commit. A clause added inside a rule gets its reason added
to that row.

**The table is checked against the prompt.** `tests/test_gen_prompt_budget.py` reads each rule's number and
heading (its opening sentence, up to the first full stop, without it) from the prompt, and each row's `#` and
`Heading` from this table, and fails unless the two lists are equal. So a rule added without a row fails, a row
left behind for a deleted rule fails, and renumbering, reordering or rewording a heading fails until the row
moves with it.

**What a rule is.** Interpretation only (what to conclude, how firmly, in what order, what to recommend). Code owns
the shape (the schema sent as `response_format`), every number, and the report's first screen: its verdict line, its
scoreboard and both its charts. Whether a rule binds is measured by evaluating the
report writer, never by checking that a sentence is present.

## The rules

Reasons come from the private campaign history the engine was extracted from (summarised, not published) and from
this package's git log. "Reason not recorded" means no source states why; do not invent one.

| # | Heading | Rule | Failure it prevents | Why |
|---|---------|------|---------------------|-----|
| 1 | RANK ON TELEMETRY, NOT JUDGE SCORES | Rank on telemetry, not judge scores: goal checks first, latency on the mean with the tail beside it; a judged comparison stands only where an aspect's `contrasts` separate it; an absolute judged claim needs a `calibrated` tier; quote bar `decision`s, never recompute them | A memo headlined "no quality was measured" over a fully judged campaign; memos written without one mechanical verdict | Judge bias mostly cancels across arms but not in an absolute claim, so comparisons are reportable before calibration and absolute claims are not. Later clauses: an unproven goal check may pass for doing nothing (a do-nothing candidate printed "passed 8/8" unmarked); no p95 below 13 observations (no order statistic is median-unbiased there); tiers decided on confidence bounds (point estimates over-awarded); an `undecided` bar is neither cleared nor failed (#593). The `verdict_order` clause: reason not recorded |
| 2 | A SHORTER TAIL CAN BE BOUGHT BY GIVING UP | Every latency claim carries the delivery rate for the same arms; `n_zero` is a proxy for declines, not a count | An arm bought a shorter tail by declining work, and quality was called "unhurt" while its failure rate went from 0% to 13% | A duration over delivered turns cannot see work that was never done |
| 3 | RANK ON THE SCOPE THAT MATCHES WHAT WAS TUNED | Rank on the scope that matches what was tuned; a scope divergence is its own finding that bounds the ranking one; check a model-call swing against provider throughput | A subsystem change ranked on whole-turn latency picked an arm whose subsystem had not moved and whose failures had risen; a ~205 s swing read as a lever effect was provider throughput drifting across sequential arms | A whole-run measure cannot attribute a movement to the part under test. The `p_adjusted` / `not_separated` clause follows the divergence test that replaced "one moved, one did not" (Gelman & Stern). The `level_a`→`level_b` ordering clause: reason not recorded |
| 4 | A LEVER THAT DID NOT MOVE THE METRIC AND A COMPARISON THAT COULD NOT RESOLVE ARE DIFFERENT FINDINGS | An established null and an unresolved comparison are different findings; no null from overlapping intervals; only `equivalent` says alike, and code's `unclear` reading is unresolved, never unchanged; `inert` / `unchecked` mechanisms qualify a null; a monotone ordering is weak evidence, never "no trend" | A 33% monotone reduction published as an absence, with a decision to stop tuning the lever | The two findings lead to opposite actions. Later: a lever that never took effect read as a clean null (#577), hence the mechanism check; `equivalent` only via TOST against a declared margin; a campaign with no declared question corrects one campaign-wide family instead of licensing uncorrected separations |
| 5 | COUNT CASES, NOT ATTEMPTS | Count cases, not attempts: separation rests on `n_cases`; intervals are already over cases; an arm's planned `k` is not its delivered repeats | 5 cases × k=3 posed as 15 independent draws; a coverage floor of 1 read as "every arm ran once" while two arms ran k=3 | Repeats of one case are not independent draws (#590 made every interval cluster-aware, so the writer must not recompute over `n`). The bundle states repeats twice, planned and delivered, and the rule had named only one |
| 6 | CONFIDENCE IS A TIER GRADED BY SUPPORT, AND THE RANGE IS THERE TO BE USED | Confidence is a tier graded by support, the range is used, and one clean result never reaches `high` | A false null was published citing the old "one clean result, direction not in doubt" anchor | The anchor licensed high confidence on a single result |
| 7 | THE DESIGN DECIDES WHAT A COMPARISON MEANS; READ `design` BEFORE MAKING ONE, AND NEVER INFER A DESIGN IT DOES NOT STATE | The design decides what a comparison means: contrasts against the control by name, never pooling contrasts that moved different levers; a confound qualifies a claim but may not name a cause; rig confounds first; config provenance; model roles; the control under an earlier identity predicate is disclosed, never contrasted, ranked or held against the control | Pooled contrasts that moved different levers; an inherited value reported as a choice; a confound read as a cause | Thin evidence is the common case, and each clause follows a misread comparison. Later confound kinds: a reasoning-effort word does not hold reasoning fixed across models (#576); one requested model id answered by several served models (#684); a fold no second arm could refute is unverified, not ruled out. The `earlier_control` clause arrived with the control split (`.22`): a bundle may carry the control's cells under an earlier identity predicate beside its arm, and a writer told only to compare against the control by name would read them as a rival arm and report the control "not separated" from itself; no memo is recorded misreading it. The cell definition that clause replaced repeated the preamble's |
| 8 | STATE EVERY FINDING IN THE DIRECTION THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS | State every finding in the direction the evidence supports | A refuted claim published at a low tier reads as support to a skimming reader | A low tier does not reverse a claim's direction |
| 9 | CONCLUSIONS ARE THE PRODUCT, WRITTEN FOR A PERSON | Conclusions are the product, written for a person: conclusion first, validity gates first; a finding's title claims no more than its evidence, names only arms it cites a reading or trial of, and never calls an unresolved contrast "no change"; a finding names the trials it is drawn from; exploratory findings say so; a short plain style guide | A headline naming the deciding setting, unusable for configuring the system; a shift between two failure modes framed as improvement; a 25-word headline of config values, a jargon summary, unread answer paragraphs; a headline saying halving a token budget "does not change" latency, cost or delivery over a contrast its own findings left unresolved (a production memo, 2026-10); a memo headlined "keep the current model" over a run that lost 10 of 12 trials to a truncated judge (G2, 2026-10); a finding headlined "four cases cannot statistically confirm the drop" under a keep the declared rule had settled on that drop (G2, 2026-10); a title joining a result to its guardrail ("Fanout 2 is faster, at no cost in delivery"), the hard negative on which all 8 shortlisted judges failed `reporter.one_idea_per_title` at calibration (Discodon, 2026-10); a finding titled "GPT-6 Luna Pro and Ling 3.1 Flash quadrupled evaluation cost" over readings at two other arms, Ling's cost shown nowhere (a production memo, 2026-10) | Owner reviews: "very strong on evidence and very weak on conclusions"; "be clear, don't overly hedge, be human". Later: "about the same" removed from the style list because it invites turning `not_separated` into "no difference"; exploratory labelling of findings resting on unasked readings (`reading_scope`). The headline and summary clauses are gone (`.26`): the report's first screen is code's verdict line and scoreboard, written from the bundle, because the G2 memo's headline decided what its own evidence could not; the null check moved onto finding titles (`analysis/null_claims.py`), and `trials` lets a finding point at the failures it names. The style block was rewritten (`.29`) after the owner read the first production report: "excessively wordy", meta-prose about the page, the question repeated, a hedge ("no measurable cost reduction") on a cost that had measurably risen. It now states the rules `analysis/style.py` lints, and the report drops, replaces or lists every sentence that breaks one (`analysis/report/memo_style.py`). `.30` adds one line: never dispute a decision rule code settled, and scope doubt to how large the difference is, after the owner read that headline as disputing the verdict; code replaces such a sentence with its own counts. A clause (#808) states the one-claim-per-title rule the judge grades but the writer was never told: a result and its guardrail, or a result and its cause, are two claims, the decision (for a cause, the finding's body) is where they meet, and a qualifier of scope, condition or size stays inside the one claim; on review the label was wrong, not the judges. The first wording kept a reason inside the claim; a Critic found that self-contradictory, since a reason needs a reading of its own, so size replaced reason and the cause joined the guardrail (owner-approved, 2026-10-16). A clause (#829) says a title names only arms it cites a reading or trial of, after a title named an arm its readings table did not show, a grounding miss written with the one-claim clause already stored; the generator refuses such a title, naming the arm (`analysis/arm_mentions.py`), so the clause tells the writer the rule the refusal enforces rather than leaving it to learn it from a paid repair |
| 10 | A CAVEAT QUALIFIES THE FINDING IT HANGS ON, AND ITS ORDER IS A PRIORITY | A caveat qualifies the finding it hangs on, ordered by what a decision-maker must know first, typed by `kind`; a re-diagnosis is the finding, not a caveat | A caveat that changed the diagnosis sat under the verdict it contradicted, on the wrong finding | A caveat placed elsewhere is not read with the claim it changes. The `immaterial` clause arrived with the materiality threshold, telling the writer an immaterial movement carries no caveat; its motivating failure is not recorded |
| 11 | THE RECOMMENDATION IS ONE ARM OR NONE, AND THE CAMPAIGN'S KIND SAYS WHICH | The campaign's kind, derived from its arms, says what the memo may recommend: a measurement nothing; a choice an arm, or none (no clear winner, which may still pick at low confidence on cost or simplicity), naming in `behind_on` every aspect another arm is shown ahead on; a change a candidate with a confirmatory separated upside, or the control. Never over a `breached` guardrail, an `undecided` one named; an exploratory change keeps the control; every warning on the recommended arm is addressed (`floors_overridden`, `bars_missed`); only keeping the control may rest on no finding; one summary per aspect | "Stop sweeping X" filed under Rejected; "Adopt A" beside "Reject B" for one choice; generic revisit triggers; an arm marked adopted that no comparison had separated from the control; "Keep the current model — adopted" in a campaign that declared no question, which the arm table then printed as the control being the "winner" (G2, 2026-10); seven single-template campaigns over one decision each wrote its own memo, so no memo recommended across them (discodon, 2026-10) | Each is a recommendation a reader cannot act on cleanly. An `undecided` guardrail names itself rather than refusing adoption: with no margin a guardrail reads held only about 2.5% of the time under no change, so refusing on undecided would refuse nearly every adoption. `.71` (one memo per campaign; owner: code reports, the writer recommends, a person decides) replaced the decision list with one recommendation, so the proposal-and-verdict, one-choice-one-decision and deferred-trigger clauses went with the decisions they governed (a deferral is now keeping the control, with what would settle it in `next`), and the question answers became one summary per aspect. The floor clause is new: floors only warn, and the owner ruled the writer must address a broken one, so the generator refuses a recommendation of an arm with a broken floor that does not name it and why (`_reject_unaddressed_warnings`). The exploratory, separation and `rests_on` clauses are refused by the generator, structurally. `.71` campaign kinds (owner, 2026-10-18): a campaign is a measurement (one arm), a choice (arms, no control) or a change (a control and candidates), derived from its arms, never declared. The no-control clause the first `.71` draft added ("with no control, recommend the arm you judge best") is gone: it let a campaign with no control recommend any arm on no evidence. A choice's pick now answers to code's pair readings (`behind_on`) and a missed absolute bar to `bars_missed`, both refused by the generator like a broken floor (`_reject_unaddressed_warnings`); a measurement recommending anything is refused, and so is a choice's pick of an arm code shows ahead on no pair at more than `low` confidence (`_reject_recommendation_off_kind`). Only `confirmatory` evidence licenses a switch, so a reading on an aspect asked after its runs is a lead (the generator counts no other) |
| 12 | NEXT EXPERIMENTS ARE ORDERED BY LEVERAGE, AND LEVERAGE IS ABOUT A DECISION | Next experiments are ordered by leverage, and leverage is about the recommendation; code's `cases_to_settle` and the page size and price a step, never the writer | The top-ranked next step was the run that would reopen the decision the memo had just adopted; a next step proposing "30 cases … for roughly $0.40" where settling needed several times that (character campaign, 2026-10) | An experiment that could overturn an adopted decision shows that decision was really deferred. The cases that would settle a rule are computed from its interval (`decision.rule_projection`); a writer asked for a rough cost guessed it, and the style gate now replaces a guess. `.71`: the page's "To switch table" and `decision.rule_projection` are gone with decision rules; an unclear aspect's cases to settle are code's (`readings[].cases_to_settle`, from the aspect's margin), and the preamble now says the writer never states a sample size or cost of its own (#846) |
| 13 | CHECK YOUR WORK, THEN CUT IT, BEFORE YOU RETURN IT | Check your work, then cut it: every claim true of all it names, every tier earned, every named cause checked against its obvious rival, every swept axis named; then cut by the per-sentence test the preamble states, with its usual failures listed as applications of it; three to five findings and two or three next steps; a rig fact that changes no conclusion is a caveat; be subject-agnostic | ~2k-word memos with 6–7 findings; a turn-limit finding in every memo; "worse across all models" when one was unchanged; a slowdown blamed on a change when that run started later; high-confidence adoption on 9–12 trials | Owner review: exhaustive writing was counter-productive. It replaced the old "record what the campaign taught about measuring" rule, which produced the turn-limit findings, and kept its axis-naming and subject-agnostic clauses. The output-cap rival named in the cause check: reason not recorded. The cut once listed only what to delete (repeats, idle caveats, idle next steps), so a sentence of a kind the list did not name (a restated method, a narrated chart, a hedge in a finding's body) passed; it now applies the general test (pacepace/3tears#682) |

## The campaign rewrite: what changed, rule by rule

One memo per campaign: the writer reads a `CampaignBundle` (every aspect's evidence, the floors, code's readings across
aspects) as a curated, budgeted view, and writes one recommendation, findings across aspects, one summary per aspect
and next steps. The rules keep their substance; what moved is the field names the writer reads and the decision
vocabulary. **This is a writer-quality change**: a deployment's stored `eval_analysis_gen` prompt still carries the
0.70 text, which names fields the writer is no longer shown and asks for decisions and answers the schema no longer
has, so every host pushes the new seed to its prompt store, and a reporter eval measures the rewrite.

| Rule | What changed |
|---|--------------|
| preamble | The bundle is a campaign bundle of aspects, and states the campaign's `kind`, a choice's `pairs`, every arm's `bars` and a measurement's `measured` readings; "code reports, you recommend, a person decides"; WHAT THE BUNDLE HOLDS names the writer view's fields (`campaign`, `arms`, `floors`, `aspects[]` with `question`, `readings`, `scoreboard`, `design`, `runs`, `cells`, `measures`, `contrasts`, `guardrails`, `bars`, `trials`, `disclosures`, `trimmed`); the run summaries, telemetry rollup, frontier and comparison lenses are no longer sent, so their sentences went; cells and trials are named uniquely across aspects; the writer never states a sample size or cost of its own |
| Rule 1 | `verdict_order`'s clause kept (it lists for the question the bars on the axes it asks about); `multiple_comparisons` → `contrasts` / `contrasts_withheld`; `bar_adjudications` → `bars`; `evidence_tier` → `tier`; the frontier clause went with the frontier (not sent); `judge_agreement` / `judge_self_agreement` named in words; "only an empty `judged_measures`" → "only cells with no judged readings"; the `max` clause went (cells carry the bad tail only) |
| Rule 2 | Unchanged |
| Rule 3 | "description" → "meaning" (the `measures` entry); `absent_scopes`, `scope_divergences` and `whole_swings` now sit in an aspect's `disclosures` |
| Rule 4 | "Where it carries no family, nothing is separated" kept; `multiple_comparisons` → `contrasts`, families in `contrast_families`; "a live question" → "the aspect's question"; adds that code's `unclear` reading is unresolved, never unchanged |
| Rule 5 | `n_independent` → `n_cases`; `run_summaries[].k_runs` → `runs[].k_runs`; `repeats_per_case_min` / `_max` → `repeats_per_case`; `sem` dropped (the view carries intervals) |
| Rule 6 | Unchanged |
| Rule 7 | "marginal over the campaign" → "over the aspect"; `arms` / `shared_levels` moved to the preamble; an arm is named by its `arm` label |
| Rule 8 | "proposals" → "reasons" |
| Rule 9 | "Order findings by decision relevance, across aspects"; style: "a title or an answer" → "a title or a summary"; the stale "keep, switch and unanswerable must agree with code's verdict line, or code replaces your sentence" (code no longer replaces those sentences) merged into "never dispute a reading or a floor code settled"; `reading_scope` is per aspect; "the decision" → "the recommendation" in the one-claim clause |
| Rule 10 | "the finding whose recommendation it would change" → "whose conclusion it would change" |
| Rule 11 | Rewritten under a new heading, then again for the three campaign kinds: one recommendation of one arm or none, not a list of decisions; aspect summaries replace question answers; the broken-floor, `behind_on`, `bars_missed` and `confirmatory` clauses are new; the first draft's no-control clause is removed (row 11) |
| Rule 12 | "confirm or overturn an adopted or rejected decision" → "overturn the recommendation"; the stale "the page's To switch table sizes and prices it" → code's `cases_to_settle` and the page |
| Rule 13 | "every decision agrees with its findings … deferred, not adopted" → "the recommendation agrees with its findings … not made; keep the control"; "an answer" → "a summary" in the axis check |

The prompt fell from 24,343 to 23,771 characters, still 13 rules.

## Not rules, but load-bearing

- **The per-sentence pruning test** (preamble, under "WRITE LESS"): every sentence must change what the reader, who
  decides what to change next, does or believes. Rule 13's cut applies it. Stated once as the general test because
  a list of things to cut passes every sentence of a kind the list does not name (pacepace/3tears#682).
- **"Every number a reader sees is code's"** (preamble; formerly a rule). It prevents fabricated citations and
  hand-typed figures nobody could find in the evidence. A finding that cites no reading has empty evidence.
- **The figure-reference grammar is appended by the generator** (`prose_refs.reference_grammar`), written from the
  parser's constants. A copy kept in a stored prompt went on teaching each deployment the grammar it was seeded
  with after the parser changed, and every generation there was refused at full cost.
- **No worked WRONG/RIGHT examples.** A model reproduced a prompt's canonical answer with its nouns swapped.
